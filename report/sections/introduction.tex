\section{Introduction}
Accurate short-term traffic forecasts underpin route guidance, ramp metering,
signal-plan selection, incident response and travel-time estimation in Intelligent
Transportation Systems~\cite{vlahogianni2014}. Forecasting freeway speed is hard
for four reasons, all visible in real sensor data: the dynamics are strongly
\emph{non-linear} near the onset of congestion; the series are \emph{seasonal}
(daily and weekly demand cycles); congestion \emph{propagates spatially} along the
road network; and loop detectors frequently \emph{fail}, leaving missing readings.

A natural engineering answer is to fit one well-tuned model per road segment, using
that segment's own history plus calendar features. Such models are cheap, robust and
easy to maintain, but by construction they cannot see a queue forming one
interchange downstream. Graph neural networks (GNNs) treat the sensor network as a
graph~\cite{li2018dcrnn,yu2018stgcn,wu2019gwnet}. Published comparisons, however,
usually pit them against feature-poor baselines and rarely test whether the gains
are statistically significant.

This paper asks a focused question: \emph{does network-aware graph learning improve
on independent road-segment forecasts, and where?} We study 5--60-minute speed
forecasting on the METR-LA benchmark~\cite{li2018dcrnn} in two phases.
\textbf{Phase~1} built a data-quality analysis, a leakage-tested feature set and a
ladder of classical and deep forecasters (up to Graph WaveNet).
\textbf{Phase~2}, the focus of this version, contributes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A strong independent-segment baseline and a test of its gap.} We fit
207 multi-output XGBoost models, one per segment, and show on out-of-fold training
residuals that their errors are spatially correlated and predictable from
neighbours' state at forecast time. This is the information an independent model
cannot use.
\item \textbf{ST-GAT}, a spatio-temporal graph attention network over the
\emph{fixed} road graph. Separate attention over downstream and upstream neighbours
respects the direction of traffic. Outage-aware masked inputs and cyclic time
encodings replace the zero-filled inputs of earlier pipelines.
\item \textbf{A technically justified hybrid.} Graph attention learns a residual
correction to the per-segment XGBoost forecasts, which are made leakage-free by
blocked, purged cross-fitting.
\item \textbf{Ablations} that isolate the value of the graph, of attention versus
mean aggregation, of the road-graph prior, of outage masking, of the residual
connection and of cross-fitting, all under equal training budgets.
\item \textbf{A fair evaluation}: chronological splits, a 12-step history for every
model, MAE/RMSE/MAPE at every horizon, peak versus off-peak and congested
regimes, Diebold--Mariano tests~\cite{diebold1995}, day-block bootstrap
intervals, congestion and attention maps, and an \emph{offline simulated}
signal-plan switching experiment. We make no claim of live traffic control.
\end{enumerate}
All code, tests and results are released in the project repository.
